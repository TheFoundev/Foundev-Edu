# Content standards

1. **Source fidelity:** use the supplied source implementation as primary evidence; distinguish implemented, configured, missing, legacy, and proposed behavior.
2. **Progression:** begin every module with a beginner-friendly, layman explanation of that module’s topic. Then move through its actual source files and named implementation symbols, explain inputs/transformations/outputs, and finish with tests, evaluation, and limitations. Scale projects to six or more modules.
3. **Standalone lessons:** include purpose/objectives, prerequisites, concept explanation, source-guided steps, code-reading example, expected behavior, exercise, debugging, good practice/security, summary, knowledge checks, and references.
4. **Exercises:** state objective, problem, requirements, constraints, behavior, acceptance criteria, hints, difficulty, and effort.
5. **Assessments:** exactly ten questions per module; four distinct options; stable IDs; 3 basic/5 intermediate/2 advanced; align to taught objectives and lessons. Keep answer indicators and explanations out of student-facing JSON.
6. **Answer keys:** one correct option per question, explain why it is correct and why every distractor is incorrect, and identify concept, lesson, and source.
7. **Final project:** include functional and non-functional requirements, milestones, deliverables, acceptance criteria, testing checklist, rubric, extensions, and mentor notes.
8. **Responsible AI:** document data provenance, split/leakage risks, metric selection, fairness, privacy, accessibility, uncertainty, and human oversight where relevant. Never represent similarity or risk scores as proof or a decision.
9. **Validation:** run the structural validator and publish the report. Automated checks cannot confirm teaching quality, external link validity, or factual accuracy; flag those for human review.
10. **Portability:** no fabricated platform IDs, secrets, application integrations, or source-project modifications. Each ZIP contains only that project's educational package.
